⚡ Track A: The 90-Second Fast Pitch

Use when time is short

Deliver this if the Board asks for a brief summary or appears to be moving quickly:

"Thank you, members of the Board. I will be very brief.

This appeal concerns parcel 30-00-72988-00-8 at 843 Winter Road in Rydal. The current assessed value is $369,690.

My opinion of fair market value as of the filing date is $989,000. Applying Montgomery County's official Common Level Ratio of 29.8%, I respectfully request that the assessed value be reduced to $294,722.

This request rests on three straightforward facts:

1. An arm's-length market purchase: I purchased this home as co-trustee in a fully arm's-length sale that settled on June 9, 2023, for exactly $989,000, as documented on the ALTA settlement statement in Exhibit C.

2. Substantially unchanged condition: No additions or structural renovations have occurred since that purchase. The property is in substantially the same physical condition, subject to normal aging.

3. Current assessment imbalance: Under the County's 29.8% CLR, the current assessment implies a market value of over $1.24 million—more than $251,000 above what the open market established.

I respectfully ask the Board to determine fair market value at $989,000 and apply the 29.8% CLR to establish an assessed value of $294,722. I am happy to answer any questions."

Use this when given the floor to present your case fully. Always use the verbal signposts to guide their eyes:

"Good [morning/afternoon], members of the Board. My name is John Yam. I am a co-trustee of the JT Winter Road Trust, record owner of 843 Winter Road in Rydal, and I reside at the property. This appeal concerns parcel 30-00-72988-00-8. The current assessed value is $369,690.

Based on that value, I respectfully request that the Board apply Montgomery County's published Common Level Ratio of 29.8% and establish an assessed value of $294,722, which represents a reduction of $74,968. If the Board finds a different fair market value, I respectfully request that it apply the same 29.8% ratio to that determined value."

"If the Board would please turn to Exhibit C on page 4 of the packet..." [Pause 3 seconds]

⏱️ Critical Phone Etiquette: The 2-Second Rule

1. The 2-Second Pause

Audio latency on teleconferences is real. When someone finishes a sentence, silently count "one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand" before answering to ensure you don't talk over them.

2. Never Speak Over Panel

If a Board member interrupts you, stop talking instantly. Yield politely: "Please go ahead, sir/ma'am."

3. Embrace Phone Silence

A 5-second silence usually means they are reading your ALTA statement or writing notes. Do not rush to fill it with nervous words.

4. Identify the Speaker

If a new voice questions you: "Thank you. May I confirm who asked that question so I can address you properly?"

🛡️ Telephonic Q&A & Rebuttal Guide (Click to Expand)

Q1: "A 2023 sale was three years ago. Why should that dictate value in 2026?"

Say this:

"I completely understand that a prior sale is not legally conclusive and that the valuation date is the July 2026 filing date. However, an actual arm's-length sale of the subject property itself is highly relevant direct evidence.

Because there have been no major improvements or structural renovations since that purchase, the physical property remains substantially identical. I offer the $989,000 sale together with the condition of the home and our submitted market context as the best available evidence of fair market value."
Q2: (From Assessor or School District) "Home values in Abington have increased since 2023. Hasn't this property appreciated?"

Say this:

"While general regional indices may show appreciation across lower- and median-priced homes in Abington, luxury and custom homes in the million-dollar range do not track general township medians.

Furthermore, under Pennsylvania law, the assessment must be grounded in specific market evidence for this property. If the County or District has documented comparable sales of similar homes supporting a higher valuation, I welcome their evidence, but general inflation assumptions should not displace the documented $989,000 market transaction."
Q3: "Did you make any renovations, additions, or major upgrades since June 2023?"

Say this (if true):

"No. There have been no additions, structural expansions, or major renovations since settlement. We have performed ordinary maintenance and repairs, but the property remains substantially as it was when purchased."
Q4: "Why did you not provide a formal certified appraisal?"

Say this:

"As a homeowner appearing pro se on behalf of our family trust, I relied on the documented arm's-length purchase price of the subject property itself, supported by the parcel records, settlement statement, and comparable area sales.

Under Pennsylvania law, an owner is competent to testify to the value of their property, and an actual arm's-length sale is direct evidence of market value."
Q5: "What about recent sales on nearby streets that sold over $1.2 million?"

Say this:

"I am aware that there are higher-priced sales in Rydal and Abington. However, those sales reflect properties with different lot topographies, different ages, newer construction, or superior finishes and amenities.

The evidence submitted in our packet—such as the recent sales on Pondview Road and Lenox Road—demonstrates that homes of this substantial size in our area have traded between $140 and $167 per square foot. Our $989,000 purchase price at approximately $163 per square foot is entirely consistent with that market reality."
Q6: "The parcel card shows 6,075 square feet. Why are you emphasizing 4,275 square feet?"

Say this:

"Because standard appraisal practice and MLS listings treat above-grade gross living area differently from finished basement area.

843 Winter Road has approximately 4,275 square feet on the first and second floors, with approximately 1,800 square feet of finished space in the lower level. Comparing 6,075 total square feet to homes that have 5,500 square feet entirely above grade would distort the valuation. Both figures are valid, but they must be applied consistently."
Q7: "What authority do you have to speak on behalf of the JT Winter Road Trust?"

Say this:

"I am a named co-trustee of the JT Winter Road Trust, which is the record owner of the property, and I reside at the home. My trustee status is reflected on the ALTA settlement statement and the recorded deed. I am appearing directly in my capacity as co-trustee."
Q8: "What if the Board finds a fair market value higher than $989,000, say $1,050,000 or $1,100,000?"

Say this:

"My opinion of value based on our purchase and condition remains $989,000. However, if the Board determines a different fair market value from the evidence, I respectfully request that the Board apply the statutory 29.8% Common Level Ratio to whatever fair market value it determines."
